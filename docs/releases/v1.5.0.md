DNSOne 1.5 adds what ISPs asked for after 1.4, and closes a hole: zone transfers.

## Security fix

Up to 1.4, dnsdist passed any zone transfer (AXFR) of a hosted zone to BIND, which allowed it because
it came from 127.0.0.1: anyone on the internet could download every hosted zone over port 53/TCP.
1.5 refuses every transfer through port 53 except from the secondaries a zone lists.

## New

- **Zone transfers to customers' own secondaries** (*Zone → Transfers*): allowed addresses or networks, NOTIFY, and an optional TSIG key per zone. Customers and resellers manage it for their zones.
- **Customer quotas**: zones an account may hold, records per zone, API requests per minute (429 with `Retry-After` above it). Resellers' customers stay within the reseller's own limits.
- **EDNS Client Subnet** for the domains you list (your CDNs), never for private or CGNAT customer addresses.
- **Serve-stale** (RFC 8767): customers keep getting a domain's last answers for up to 12 hours (configurable) when its name servers are down.
- **DNSSEC automation**: CDS and CDNSKEY shown, BIND told when the parent's DS appears or goes, DS records of a child zone kept in step automatically when its parent is hosted here too, and key rollovers (*Roll the key*) that complete by themselves.
- **Attack protection** (*Protection*): dnsdist detects random-subdomain ("water torture") attacks, infected clients and amplification victims, with a week's history and thresholds in the console; block clients or domains on every node, or a client at the firewall.
- **Firewall** (*Settings → Firewall*): nftables rules managed from the console. SSH and the console stay open to the management networks whatever the rules say, and an apply the console cannot confirm within a minute is rolled back by itself.

## Upgrading from 1.4

`sudo dnsone-update` (or *Settings → Overview → Software update*) runs `ops/dnsone-upgrade-1.5`. By hand, from the built release, on each server, the primary first:

```
sudo bash ops/dnsone-upgrade-1.5 /path/to/dnsone-src
```

The database changes are additive. The Recursor restarts once (its cache starts empty) and DNS pauses for a second or two while dnsdist restarts. If `/etc/dnsdist/dnsdist.conf` has local edits, the script stops before changing anything and shows them; merge them and rerun with `--keep-dnsdist`. The firewall stays off until you turn it on. Rollback: `sudo bash ops/dnsone-upgrade-1.5 --rollback /var/backups/dnsone/pre-1.5-….tar.gz`.

Details: `docs/RUNBOOK.md`, "From 1.4 to 1.5", "Customers and resolver (1.5)" and "Security (1.5)".
